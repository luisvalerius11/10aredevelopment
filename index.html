The honest read

Option C does not dramatically outperform Option A on pure IRR. The reasons to prefer it are: (1) two diversified revenue streams reduce single-point-of-failure risk; (2) shops can cash-flow from month 9 while wellness ramps; (3) it earns the same halo to Casa Nira at lower wellness scale; (4) it has better exit optionality if any single component underperforms. The four bear-case scenarios in the sensitivity table tell the same story — even in adverse cases, Option C remains workable, where Option A becomes binary.

Next Step 3 · Land Renegotiation Strategy

The 23-year lease is the binding constraint — and the seller's problem too.

A 23-year tenor on commercial wellness assets generates marginal IRR for any rational buyer. That weakness is leverage. Three asks, in priority order — at least one must move for the deal to make sense.

Ask 1 · Tenor extension

30 + 30 years instead of 23 + 25

This is the Uluwatu market norm for new leasehold commercial deals (Propertia 2026 benchmarks). Adds 7 productive years on the front end and 5 on the extension. Single biggest IRR lever — moves 23-yr IRR from 9.7% to ~14%.

Counter-offer position: If 30+30 is firm-no, push for 25+25 minimum.

Ask 2 · Price reduction

10–15% below current ask

Frame as a function of the commercial use case: residential and pure-villa buyers can absorb the 23-yr tenor; commercial wellness/mixed-use buyers cannot at current pricing. We are functionally the only credible buyer for a commercial use of this back-row plot.

Counter-offer position: 5% if tenor moves to 30+30.

Ask 3 · Deferred payment

Payment structured against project milestones

30% on signing, 30% at PBG (building permit) issuance, 40% at handover/completion. Reduces capital lock-up during 14–18 month build, and aligns seller incentives with our permit-getting timeline.

Counter-offer position: 50/30/20 structure if 30/30/40 rejected.

Our BATNA — what we walk away to

If seller refuses all three asks

Walk. Redeploy IDR 36–40 Bn into Option B (Casa Nira II villa cluster) on an alternative Bukit plot — there are several active 8–15 are listings in Pecatu and Bingin at IDR 550–600 M/are freehold that pencil better.

Tell the seller this explicitly during negotiation — not as a threat, but as context. Bali land is rising 8–15%/yr; the seller has a window before they need to drop price or extend tenor to attract real buyers.

Defensive value of this specific plot

Acknowledge internally: the worst outcome is not "we walk." The worst outcome is someone else buys it and builds something that hurts Casa Nira — loud F&B, ugly compound, low-grade hostel. Price the defensive value (~IDR 3–5 Bn implied) into our walk-away threshold.

Our real walk-away is therefore: total economic cost (price - defensive value) makes Option C IRR fall below ~7% even with halo.

Negotiation opener — talking script

CONTEXT: First sit-down with seller / agent · TONE: collaborative, problem-solving, not adversarial
"We've spent the last two months underwriting this land seriously, and we want to be the buyer. We have the team, the funding, and the adjacent project — no other prospective buyer offers you the speed and certainty we do. But our underwriting keeps hitting one structural problem. The 23-year primary tenor was designed for a residential leasehold buyer who pays cash and lives in the asset. For a commercial use — which is what this back-row location naturally suits — the tenor produces an IRR that no rational developer can underwrite at the current price. That's not a negotiating posture; that's the arithmetic. So we want to find a structure that works for both sides. There are three things that would move the deal across the line for us, and we can be flexible on which one moves: 1. Extend the tenor to 30+30, which is the Uluwatu commercial norm right now. 2. Or, hold the tenor at 23+25 but reduce the headline price by 10–15%. 3. Or, restructure payment against project milestones — 30% on signing, 30% at PBG, 40% at completion. Any one of these unlocks the deal for us. Two of them get us to a strong yes. We'd like to understand which is the easiest for you to move on, and build the contract around that."

Substance-based compliance — the new enforcement test

KBLI 2025 introduces a "substance over form" principle: regulators evaluate whether the company's actual operational activities match its registered KBLI and declared investment value. Implications for our plan:

  • The mixed-use building requires a multi-KBLI registration on a single PMA, OR separate PMAs for spa (96122) and F&B (56101) and leasing (68112). Cleaner structure: one HoldCo, three OpCos — each with its own NIB.
  • Virtual office addresses are no longer sufficient — each entity needs a verifiable physical operating address. We have that via the new building, but interim arrangements during construction need attention.
  • Declared investment value must match actual deployed capital. The IDR 36.7 Bn capex easily clears the IDR 10 Bn PMA floor across each OpCo, but each entity's declared investment must be defensible.

Recommended entity structure

HoldCo (existing Aurum PMA, KBLI 68112 / 70209) → owns the leasehold land title and the building.

Three sub-tenant OpCos (new PMAs):

  • SpaCo (KBLI 96122) — operates the recovery floor; rents space from HoldCo at arm's-length.
  • F&BCo (KBLI 56101) — operates the café; rents space from HoldCo.
  • LeaseCo (KBLI 68112) — sub-leases shop units to retail tenants.

This separates risk profiles and license risk. If one OpCo loses its license, the others continue. HoldCo's rental income from sub-tenants is stable, predictable, and easier to value for any future capital event.
